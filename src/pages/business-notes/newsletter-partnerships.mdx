## The action point

Do not sell a newsletter placement as a one-off slot. Sell a **small, time-bound partnership** to a carefully selected company: a clear story, useful work made around it, and repeated exposure across a defined period.

That changes the conversation from “what does one ad cost?” to “what will this partnership help our audience understand, try, or do?”

## How to price it

- **Start with the audience, not the send.** Be able to explain who reads, what they care about, and why this company is relevant to them.
- **Price the whole outcome.** Include strategy, editorial thinking, creation, distribution, and the right to reuse the resulting work—not just impressions or a logo in an email.
- **Create a fixed season or cohort.** Define a period, a limited number of partners, and what appears in each issue. Scarcity is more credible when capacity is genuinely limited.
- **Set a floor that makes the work worth doing.** A partnership should cover the time needed to make something useful and distinctive. If it only pays for a mention, it will become a mention.
- **Use an early-partner rate deliberately.** A first-season discount can reduce buyer risk, but keep the standard future price visible so the discount has a clear boundary.
- **Keep the offer simple.** One core package, a clear timeframe, and a few examples of possible deliverables are easier to buy than a long menu of placements.

## Choose customers, do not just accept sponsors

The strongest partner is not simply the company with budget. It is one with:

1. A real fit with the audience.
2. A product or point of view worth exploring.
3. A story that can become useful editorial work rather than an advert.
4. A team that can collaborate quickly and give enough access, proof, or data.
5. A reputation you are comfortable lending your own credibility to.

Say no when the product is weak, the audience match is vague, or the work would make the newsletter less useful. Each partnership teaches readers what future recommendations mean.

## A simple offer to return to

> **A limited partnership for companies that genuinely fit the readership.** Over a defined period, we develop a useful story or artifact around the company’s work and share it consistently with the audience. The partner receives the finished work to reuse; readers receive something worth their attention.

## Before sending a proposal

- Can I describe the audience in one specific sentence?
- Does this company have a story readers would value even without a sales pitch?
- What useful thing could we make—not just say?
- Is the scope limited enough to protect quality?
- Is the price high enough to protect attention and effort?
- Would I be happy to show this partnership as an example a year from now?
